# Nexus 1.18.0

The headline is contesting from the keyboard. Enter Sends Message works in the CW, RTTY and Phone
cockpits as it does in N1MM Logger+: each Enter sends the contact's next message and the last one
logs it. It is off until you turn it on. As you type a call, Super Check Partial lists the calls
active in recent contests, and an imported call history file fills in the exchange. The contest
F-keys follow N1MM's layout, Ctrl+D twice takes back the last contact, Ctrl+Y fixes one in place,
and at the Illinois QSO Party a station on a county line takes one entry and a mobile's **I moved**
changes the county you send. A second person can log at a second monitor and keyboard in the contest
logger window, which shares the contact you are both typing and transmits nothing, and the Contest
screen takes contacts in the same large log line. Everything under that line can pop out to a
window of its own. Field Day mode is now **Contest mode**, the one switch for whichever contest you
pick. Every contest log strip shows the running score, and turning the mode off offers to merge the
log, export it and put back what the contest changed. CQ WW and CQ WPX Cabrillo files carry the
category, club, name and email lines CQ asks for.

Club sync runs the contest you picked under that contest's rules, the Illinois QSO Party included,
with a scoreboard made for a TV across the room and each position's clock shown against the host's.
A joining laptop says whether it is in, a button lets Nexus through Windows Firewall, and Ctrl+D
takes a contact back from the whole club. The host can give a club position to the laptop it
belongs to. The club board shows every position in a band × mode grid, and the host sees where
each position's link stands and can remove a position and let it back. Every laptop in a club needs
this version, the host's too. Winter Field Day runs on the sponsor's 2027 rules, with its
objectives and the score they make.

Panes move when you drag them with the mouse, and the Phone, CW, JS8 and FT screens take boxes of
any Conditions pane. The Rotor box points the antenna at the station in your log entry. The status
bar names the problem instead of RADIO STOPPED, Nexus shares wfview's rigctld, and six Icom radios
connect over your network (Beta, receive and control only). LoTW brings your whole confirmation
history, and each confirmation lands on the contact it confirms. In FT8 and FT4, Decode no longer
holds the transmitter keyed, a mode switch in the middle of a QSO turns TX off when your Tx
frequency leaves the waterfall, each contest sends its own messages, and Fake It split puts the
dial back after every over, as WSJT-X does. After Stop TX, TX stays off until you turn it back on,
whichever screens you visit.

## The short version

- **Enter Sends Message (ESM)** in the CW, RTTY and Phone cockpits, as N1MM Logger+ has it, off
  until you turn it on. Enter never turns TX on.
- **Super Check Partial and call history** in every contest's log strip.
- **The contest F-keys follow N1MM's layout.** F3 is TU in CW, and AGN is F8 in CW, RTTY, PSK and
  Phone.
- **Ctrl+D twice takes back the last contest contact**, at a club too, and Restore puts it back.
- **Ctrl+Y fixes a contest contact in place**, and **Edit** does it for any row of the Contest
  screen's log (not at a club yet). **Alt+W** wipes the log line, **Ctrl+O** goes to the operator
  box, and with Enter Sends Message a dupe that calls your run gets QSO B4.
- **A contest logger window** for a second person at a second monitor and keyboard: one contact,
  typed in either window and logged once, and nothing in that window transmits.
- **The Contest screen takes contacts** in the same large log line, with the Operator box on its
  title line, and **⧉ Pop out** puts everything under it in a window of its own, for another
  monitor. **Each exchange box says what it holds**: County / State, IL county, Serial, Grid and so
  on, where the county box said QTH.
- **Contest mode**, the one switch for whichever contest you pick (it was Field Day mode). A new
  pick takes effect when you save it, every contest log strip shows the running score, and turning
  the mode off offers a "Contest over?" checklist.
- **Club sync for any contest it can run**, the Illinois QSO Party included, with a TV scoreboard
  and each position's clock against the host's. A joining laptop says whether it is in, a button
  lets Nexus through Windows Firewall, a click on an event Find club events found joins it, and the
  host can give a club position to the laptop it belongs to, after checking its club code. The club
  board shows every position in a band × mode grid, and the host sees each position's link and can
  remove a position and let it back. Every laptop needs this version.
- **Illinois QSO Party:** a county-line station in one entry (its counties joined by `/` or `+`),
  **I moved** for a mobile, which works in every QSO party, a dupe warning that names the counties
  of a county line already worked, and the Cabrillo header lines the sponsor's software reads.
- **Cabrillo and N3FJP:** Settings ▸ Contesting reads in the order you set up an entry, and **Your
  entry** adds the multi-operator class, overlay, mailing address and soapbox a sponsor's template
  asks for. The Summary has a band by mode table, and N3FJP gets the exchange in CQ WPX, CQ WW RTTY
  and the ARRL VHF contests.
- **CQ WW and CQ WPX Cabrillo files** carry the category, club, name and email lines CQ asks for,
  each from your Settings, with a new **Band category** in Your entry. A line you leave blank is
  left out, and the Contest screen names one CQ requires before you export. CQ WW SSB is on 24 and
  25 October.
- **Contest logs:** a contest's log holds the whole contest, so a log exported weeks later is
  complete, a contact you never merged is never lost, merging one into your logbook never adds a
  contact twice, and each contact keeps the exchange it was sent with. A dupe you log is kept,
  marked, for 0 points, as N1MM Logger+ does.
- **Winter Field Day 2027:** 23 and 24 January, the thirteen objectives and their score, and no
  spots over the internet while it runs.
- **Arrange your screen:** drag panes, add boxes of any Conditions pane in Phone, CW, JS8 and FT,
  and a Needed box. Nothing moves until you move it.
- **Rotor and Amplifier:** Point turns the antenna to the station in your log entry, and both boxes
  draw their readings bigger in the dashboard rail.
- **Radios:** the status bar names the problem, Nexus shares wfview's rigctld and tries CAT again
  on its own, and the Icom network connection (Beta, receive and control only, not yet tried on a
  radio). Fake It split puts the dial back after every over, and Rig split on Nexus's own Icom
  CI-V puts the TX VFO in USB with DATA for FT8 (neither yet checked on a radio).
- **Confirmations:** your whole LoTW history, each confirmation on the right contact, and
  **Check confirmations** in the Logbook for the ones earlier versions put on the wrong one.
- **FT8 and FT4:** Decode no longer holds the transmitter keyed, the decodes of the period after a
  band change show as in WSJT-X, a mode switch turns TX off when your Tx frequency leaves the
  waterfall, and a contest works as in WSJT-X: its own messages, and Call CQ, Tx 6, S&P, a
  double-click and a logger's reply all in the contest.
- **Nexus Remote:** the stream tries only addresses a viewer could really be at, the Contest
  screen's exports, Restore and Merge are refused through it, and the page says "Station data
  unavailable" after every loss of the station's data.
- **Outside contests:** after Stop TX, TX stays off until you turn it back on, whichever screens you
  visit, and Stop TX ends an RTTY Auto run; no CW ID after an FT8 or FT4 contact, a Phone keyer card
  for F8 AGN, and the Needed window following your New DXCC bands (What changes outside contests,

- **Also:** solar wind updates again, Repair clock shows only when the clock is really off, a QRZ
  pull that finds nothing new is no longer blamed on your API key, loading a profile no longer
  switches your uploads, ClubLog uploads work on Linux and the Raspberry Pi, "New mode" counts

  map labels read in the light theme, the Needed window keeps to your New grid bands, and

## Upgrading from 1.17

- **The CW contest F-keys moved.** In every contest F1 is your CQ, F2 his call and your exchange,
  F3 TU and your call, F4 your call, F5 his call, F6 your exchange in search and pounce, F7 QSO B4
  and F8 AGN. Only F1 sends what it did, and AGN moved from F7 to F8, so habit is the risk: F3
  pressed for the exchange now sends TU, F4 pressed to confirm sends only your call, F5 pressed for
  your call sends his, and F7 pressed for a repeat sends QSO B4.
  The key captions say what each one sends. A macro profile of your own keeps its texts; if you
  copied the old contest keys into one, add `{RST}` before `{EXCH}` in its F3 and F4 so they send
  your report. In the RTTY and PSK Contest keys F7 and F8 trade the same way (F7 B4, F8 AGN), and a
  key you changed there moves with its message. In Phone the voice keyer has a new slot, F8 AGN,
  which Enter Sends Message plays, and F5 keeps what you recorded there.
- **Field Day mode is now Contest mode.** The switch is at the top of the Contest screen and in
  Settings ▸ Contesting ▸ Contest, under the contest picker, and no longer in Settings ▸ Appearance ▸
  Features. Your setting carries over. With it on, saving a different contest switches at once: the running
  contest ends, a CQ run with it, and the new one starts in S&P without calling CQ.
- **The contest exports are named for the contest**: `ilqp-log-<date>.cbr` at the Illinois QSO
  Party, `arrlfd-log-<date>.cbr` at ARRL Field Day, and the club's `ilqp-club-log-<date>.cbr`, where
  every contest's file was `fd-log-…`.
- **Club sync: update every laptop, the host's too.** A host on this version turns away a laptop on
  an older Nexus, by name, and an older host turns this one away. Contacts logged meanwhile stay in
  that laptop's log and go up when it joins. On each Windows laptop, press **Allow Nexus through
  Windows Firewall** in Settings ▸ Contesting.
- **Settings ▸ Spots & Alerts shows what the Needed boards follow.** With the new DXCC and grid
  alert off, New DXCC, New grid and Rare grid 💎 all read Off, though the boards followed the band
  choices saved underneath. They now show those choices, and **New DXCC and grid alerts** is a switch
  of its own. If rare HF squares show on the Needed board and you do not want them, set Rare grid 💎
  to VHF+. The Needed window and the dashboard window's Needed box now follow New DXCC as well, as
  the docked board does: at Off, HF only or VHF+ they leave out new DXCC stations on other bands. Set
  New DXCC to All bands to see every one.
- **Contest contacts merged by an earlier version** keep their names in your logbook and are
  recognised, so merging again adds only contacts never merged. A contact an earlier version added
  twice stays twice until you delete one copy. A contest contact logged with an earlier version
  that never saved the exchange it was sent with takes what Settings say the first time this
  version opens that contest, and keeps it.
- **LoTW: press Download everything again once** (Settings ▸ Logging & Connectors ▸ Confirmations ▸
  LoTW) if you set up LoTW in Nexus before this release. The first download asked for too little,
  so older confirmations never arrived. If you ever work a station twice on one band in a day, run
  **Check confirmations** in the Logbook too.
- **"New mode" counts each mode you log on its own**, as the callsign card always did. A country
  you worked on FT4 shows as a new mode on FT8, so expect more mode needs than before. A station
  heard in WSPR, or another mode no contact is logged in, is still a new mode only when you have
  never worked its country in any mode of that kind.
- **Loading a profile leaves your upload switches alone.** QRZ, ClubLog, eQSL, LoTW and the rest
  stay as you last set them, and so do the switches that send contacts to another logging program.
- **A mode switch in the middle of a QSO can turn TX off.** Between FT8, FT4, FT2, Tempo Fast and
  Tempo Deep, when your Tx frequency is off the waterfall at the new dial, the over stops and TX
  turns off, as WSJT-X does. Turn TX on again to carry on.
- **In an FT8/FT4 contest, the FT cockpit works the contest.** While a contest runs, Call CQ calls
  the contest's CQ (`CQ FD` at ARRL Field Day), S&P is the contest's S&P, and a double-click on a CQ
  or a reply from JTAlert or GridTracker works the station into the contest log. After a contact you
  started with a double-click, TX goes off when Disable TX after sending 73 is on. The Tx6 row
  shows the contest's CQ at ARRL Field Day and in the ARRL VHF contests, and Tx 6 calls it. In the
  other contests that take FT8, Call CQ and Tx 6 send the CQ in the Tx6 row, so a `CQ DX` left there
  goes out as `CQ DX`.
- **A contest's log holds the whole contest.** A station you worked days ago in the same contest is
  a dupe, and a contest contact you never merged stays in this computer's contest journal until it is
  in your logbook, where the Contest screen offers to merge it.
- **A dupe you log is kept, for 0 points.** At ARRL Field Day, Winter Field Day and the California,
  Illinois, Ohio, Tennessee and Texas QSO parties, Enter on a dupe in the CW, Phone or RTTY log strip
  logged nothing. It now logs the contact, marked as a dupe and worth no points, as N1MM Logger+
  does, and the dupe warning still shows while you type. With Enter Sends Message on, a dupe that
  calls your run gets QSO B4 once and the next Enter logs it; in search and pounce, Enter on a dupe
  still sends nothing and logs nothing.
- **After Stop TX, TX stays off when you change screens.** After Stop TX, Esc, TX Off, a Halt in
  FT8 or the watchdog, coming back to Phone, CW, RTTY or PSK leaves TX off until you turn it on:
  TX On, a CW F-key, RTTY's TX button, or a first PTT press in Phone, which turns TX on without
  keying, so press again to talk. Stop TX and Esc also end an RTTY Auto run.
- **Settings ▸ Contesting is in a new order.** The Contest mode switch is under the contest picker;
  Entry category, Power category, Unassisted entry, Email, Entry class, Club and Other operators are
  under **Your entry**; and the Field Day class, section and power multiplier are in a Field Day
  group that shows only for ARRL and Winter Field Day. Every setting keeps its value.
- **The Contest screen's Operator box is on its log line.** While a contest runs it sits on the log
  line's title line, beside Sending and I moved, and Ctrl+O puts the cursor there; the scoreboard
  below has none. With no contest running, and in the torn-off scoreboard, the box is where it was.
- **NET rigctl with wfview: leave rigctld TCP Port as it is.** With a Network Address on this
  computer, Nexus talks to the rigctld there itself and starts none of its own in front of it. A
  setup an earlier version saved with another radio's rigctld, or the Share this radio port, on
  that address is refused until you change it: Nexus sends nothing there, and the CAT status names
  the radio or the port to change. Changing it connects at once.
- **Through the Remote stream, the Contest screen exports nothing.** Its exports, Restore and Merge
  are the station's own act now: a press through the stream does nothing and says "Only at the
  station: …". Press them at the station.
- **Your screen stays as it was.** Nothing moves and no box shows until you move or add it, each
  cockpit's dashboard rail starts as the rail you had, and Reset layout puts everything back.
- **Winter Field Day: the objectives replace ARRL Field Day's bonuses**, so a bonus ticked there
  before scores nothing.

## What changes outside contests

If you never turn Contest mode on, these are the differences you will meet.

- **No CW ID after an FT8 or FT4 contact, as in WSJT-X.** With CW ID after 73 on, Nexus no longer
  keys your call in CW after its final 73 in FT8, FT4, FT2, TempoFast or TempoDeep, so with Disable
  TX after sending 73 on, TX stays off after those contacts. Q65, FST4 and MSK144 send the ID as
  before.
- **A contest picked in Settings never blocks FT8 or Chat.** With Contest mode off, Call CQ, the Tx6
  CQ, S&P and Chat work whatever contest is picked, filled in or not, and a Call CQ that cannot start
  says why in a toast.
- **The Phone voice keyer has a seventh card, F8 AGN**, empty until you record it, and F5 keeps
  what you recorded there. F6 Again stays.
- **Phone's F-keys play the voice keyer from the log line too**, as CW's macros do: with the cursor
  in the Call box, F1 plays F1's recording. While the voice keyer is showing, F1 to F12 never reach
  the window, so F5 cannot reload Nexus, even while you record. With Alt, Ctrl or Cmd held an F-key is left to the
  system, so Alt+F4 still closes the window.
- **The RTTY and PSK Contest sets have B4 on F7 and AGN on F8**, also when you pick the Contest set
  outside a contest, and a key you changed there moves with its message. The Everyday sets are
  unchanged.
- **Settings ▸ Spots & Alerts:** New DXCC and grid alerts is a switch of its own, and the three band
  menus show what is saved.
- **The Needed window and the dashboard's Needed box follow the New DXCC menu**, as the docked board
  does: with New DXCC at Off, HF only or VHF+, new DXCC stations on the other bands are not listed.
  Set New DXCC to All bands to see every one.
- **A Needed row opens its own mode's screen:** a JS8 row the JS8 screen, a PSK row the PSK screen
  and an FT4 row the FT screen on FT4, where a JS8 or PSK row opened the FT screen. The click sends
  nothing; the PSK screen turns TX on as it does when you open it yourself, and leaves it off after
  Stop TX.
- **After Stop TX, TX stays off until you turn it back on, whichever screens you visit.** Esc, TX
  Off, a Halt in FT8 and the watchdog count as stops too. Coming back to Phone, CW, RTTY or PSK no
  longer turns TX on after one: TX On, a CW F-key, RTTY's TX button or a first PTT press in Phone
  does (that press turns TX on and keys nothing; the next one keys). A program keying through
  Nexus's shared radio port, such as WSJT-X or N1MM, cannot key either until you do. Entering a
  cockpit still turns TX on when nothing stopped it, at launch or coming from FT8 with no Halt.
- **Stop TX and Esc end an RTTY Auto run**, and so do a band change and a radio switch. Auto stays
  on: Auto CQ starts a new run.
- **Field Day mode is Contest mode**, named for the contest you pick. Its switch is no longer in
  Settings ▸ Appearance ▸ Features but under the contest picker in Settings ▸ Contesting, which now
  reads in the order you set up an entry, and ARRL Field Day's power multiplier shows only when ARRL
  Field Day is picked.
- **Settings ▸ CW ▸ Reset to defaults and Customize** also clear the steps mapped to Enter Sends
  Message.
- **The dashboard rail's Rotor and Amplifier boxes draw bigger readings.** At 1024×768, where the
  rail folds into the cockpit's column, the taller Amplifier box puts the boxes under it lower. The
  Remote page's amplifier card shows the large readings too.
- **Map labels read in the light theme**; the dark theme is unchanged.

## Contesting

- **Enter Sends Message (ESM)** in the CW, RTTY and Phone cockpits, as N1MM Logger+ has it. Each
  Enter in the contest log strip sends the contact's next message from your F-keys, and the Enter
  for the last step logs the contact. Running: CQ, then his call and your exchange, then TU, which
  logs it. Search and pounce: your call, then your exchange, which logs it. The dock shows **Run**
  or **S&P** (click it to switch), lights the key the next Enter sends, and says why when it sends
  nothing. Enter never turns TX on: after Stop TX, Esc or the watchdog it sends nothing until you
  turn TX back on yourself, and a trip to another screen and back does not turn it on either. A
  dupe of your own log that calls your run gets QSO B4 once (F7 in the CW and RTTY contest keys),
  and the next Enter logs it for 0 points; in search and pounce Enter sends nothing to a dupe, as
  in N1MM. The Phone voice keyer has no QSO B4 slot: record one and map QSO B4 to it in Settings ▸
  Contesting ▸ Enter Sends Message, or Enter sends nothing for it. Holding Enter sends once, Alt+Enter logs without sending, and a message
  you stop counts as not sent. In Phone, Enter plays the voice keyer's recordings. ESM is off until
  you turn it on, for each cockpit, with the ESM switch in the TX dock or in Settings ▸ Contesting ▸
  Enter Sends Message, where you also choose which key sends each step. It steps aside while RTTY's
  auto sequence runs or Continuous TX is latched, and it never runs in the FT modes or on the Remote
  page. Settings ▸ CW ▸ Reset to defaults clears the steps you mapped with the profile's keys.
- **AGN is F8 in the Phone, CW, RTTY and PSK contest keys, as in N1MM's default message sets**, and
  B4 is F7 in CW, RTTY and PSK. The RTTY and PSK Contest texts you changed and the steps you mapped
  for Enter Sends Message move with their messages. In Phone the voice keyer has a new slot, F8
  AGN, which Enter Sends Message plays when you need a repeat, and F5 is yours.
- **Ctrl+D twice takes back the last contest contact.** Ctrl+D, or **Remove last** beside Clear,
  names your newest contest contact on the strip, and a second press within 5 seconds takes it out
  of the score, the multipliers, the dupe check and every export. The contest screen keeps it under
  **Removed**, where **Restore** puts it back exactly. The strip says where the contact had already
  gone (N3FJP, the N1MM broadcast, WSJT-X listeners, your logbook's uploads), since Nexus cannot
  take it back from there. Ctrl+D never keys or stops the transmitter. At a club it takes the
  contact back from the club too (see Club sync). It is not on the Remote page or in the Remote
  client, and a press through the Remote stream removes nothing.
- **Ctrl+Y fixes a contest contact in place**, and every row of the Contest screen's log has
  **Edit**. Change the call, what they sent, the band or the mode, then press Enter; Esc leaves it as
  it was. The contact keeps its time, serial and number, a restart keeps the edit, and the dupe
  marks, multipliers, score, Cabrillo, ADIF and a later merge into your logbook follow it. A copy
  merged into your logbook before the edit is named, so you can fix it there too. Nothing in an
  edit transmits. Not at a club yet: there, take the contact back with Ctrl+D and log it again. Not
  through Remote.
- **Alt+W and Ctrl+O, as in N1MM.** Alt+W clears the whole contest log line, the call and every
  exchange box, and a second Alt+W right after puts it back, serial number included (Option+W on a
  Mac). Ctrl+O from any contest log strip opens the Contest screen with the cursor in its Operator
  box (in the logger window, that window's own), leaving the mode and the radio as they are.
  Neither key transmits or stops a transmission.
- **Super Check Partial and call history.** As you type a call in a contest's log strip, a line
  under it lists the calls active in recent contests that contain what you typed: calls in your log
  first, and from the fourth character the calls one character different. Click one to put it in
  the Call box. The list comes from supercheckpartial.com; Nexus downloads it the first time a
  contest starts and checks for a newer copy at most once a day. You can also import an N1MM-format
  call history file for one contest, and the exchange boxes it fills say **history**. What you type
  wins, and a report, a serial, a Field Day class or a Sweepstakes check never comes from the file.
  Settings ▸ Contesting ▸ Super Check Partial and call history turns either off, and Unassisted
  mode turns both off.
- **A contest logger window, for a second person logging at a second monitor and keyboard.** Click
  **⧉ Logger window** on the Contest screen, or in the torn-off scoreboard beside the operator box.
  Its log line is the top of the window, in large type, with the Operator box on its title line and
  the dupe warning right under the boxes, then the last five contacts, newest first, and the whole
  Contest screen below; A− / A+ makes the line and the contacts smaller or larger, and that computer
  remembers it. The log line is the same contact as your own contest strip: whatever either of you
  types shows in both as it is typed, with the same dupe warning, Super Check Partial line,
  call-history fill and Ctrl+D line, and Enter in either window logs it once, even when you both
  press it at the same moment. Nothing in the logger window transmits: it has no PTT, F-key messages
  or Tune, Space only types, and its Esc clears the log line without touching your over. Its F-keys
  do nothing, so F5 cannot reload it, while Alt+F4 closes it (not yet tried on Windows). Stop TX and
  every transmit control stay on your screen, and the contest switch, Running / S&P and scoring are
  read-only in the logger window. It logs as this computer's own position under the mode you are
  operating, so club sync and your exports are unchanged, and it reopens on the monitor where you
  left it. A Remote viewer sees the main window only. Not yet tried by two people with two
  keyboards and mice through MouseMux.
- **The Contest screen takes your contacts.** With a contest running, the top of the Contest
  screen is the logger window's large log line: the Call and exchange boxes in large type with the
  dupe warning right under them, the last five contacts, and A− / A+, which this computer remembers
  for the main window apart from the logger window. The Operator box sits on its title line, beside
  Sending and I moved: it saves the operator as before, the top bar's operator follows it, and
  Enter in it never logs a contact. It is the same log line as the cockpits' log panes, so a contact
  typed there shows in the logger window too and logs once, under the mode you are operating.
  Nothing on it transmits: no PTT, no F-key messages, no Enter Sends Message, no Tune. Esc there
  stops TX, as the top bar's Stop TX does (an FT8 or FT4 run going on behind it included), and
  leaves the boxes as they are. On a mode the contest does not permit, the line says so, and the
  warning in the screen's header shows only then. Under the last contacts, the contest controls,
  club board, score, sections and log use the whole width of a wide window: Logger window, the
  exports, Summary and Dupe sheet sit in one row, with the merge and its upload boxes beside them
  where there is room, and at 1024×768 every upload box can be reached.
- **The rest of the Contest screen pops out to a window of its own.** **⧉ Pop out**, at the right
  end of the line with the Contest mode switch, opens everything below the log line in its own
  window, to put on another monitor: the Contest mode switch, Running / S&P, the exports and the
  merge, the club board, the score, the county or sections board, the bonuses and the log, all
  working there as they do on the main screen. The main window keeps the log line with its
  Operator box (Ctrl+O still lands there) and the last contacts, and one line where the rest was,
  with **Bring it back**. Closing the window any way brings it back too, and so does restarting
  Nexus. The window opens where you last put it, fitted to the monitors you have then. Its F-keys
  do nothing, so F5 cannot reload it, and its Esc is not a stop: Esc and Stop TX in the main window
  are. Not yet tried on Windows.
- **Each exchange box says what it holds.** The box for the other station's county read QTH. It
  now reads **County / State** inside a QSO party's state and **IL county** (or NY, OH, TN, TX, CA)
  outside it, with a county from its own list as a greyed example (`COOK`). CQ WW RTTY's box reads
  State, CQ WPX's and the California QSO Party's Serial, the VHF contests' Grid, and Sweepstakes'
  Serial, Precedence, Call, Check and Section. Field Day's Class and Section and CQ WW's Zone are
  unchanged, and the logger window, the Contest screen's log and an edit use the same words. What
  is logged, sent and scored is unchanged.
- **The report follows the mode.** After a contact, a move between Phone and CW or RTTY shows the
  new mode's own report, 59 on Phone and 599 on CW, RTTY and the keyboard modes, in both windows.
  A report you typed stays with that contact,
  and the next contact, and the line after Clear, start on the mode's own. On Phone the Sending line
  and the Give line beside PTT read 59, the report a phone contact is logged with.
- **The CW contest F-keys follow N1MM's layout** (see Upgrading). With a report in the exchange, F2
  and F6 send the 5NN before your county, zone or serial. A CW F-key sends once per press however
  long you hold it, and an F-key with Ctrl, Alt or Cmd held is left to the system. The call in the
  log strip and His Call are one field, so the call you typed is the call every key sends.

- **A new pick takes effect when you save it.** With Contest mode on, saving a different contest in
  Settings ▸ Contesting ▸ Contest does exactly what turning the mode off and on did: the running

  mode was on, or when the mode went on, so a club call typed in the day before goes back to your

  Day lit, but nothing is noted until the mode goes on. With no contest picked, a change to your
  callsign, the operator or Unassisted is not noted at all.
- **FT8 and FT4 in a contest work the way WSJT-X does.** In the ARRL January, June and September
  VHF contests, Running and S&P send what WSJT-X sends in its NA VHF Contest mode (`CQ TEST`, your
  grid, `R` and your grid, RR73, and 73 from the station running), and log the other station's grid

  the California, Ohio, Tennessee and Texas QSO parties, where WSJT-X has no contest mode, they send

  the contest's Running, and so is Tx 6: at ARRL Field Day and in the VHF contests the Tx6 row shows
  the contest's CQ, and in the other contests it stays yours, so a directed CQ typed there
  (`CQ QP <your call> <grid>`, say) is what the run sends. Its S&P is the contest's S&P, and a
  double-click on a CQ, or a reply to one
  from JTAlert or GridTracker, works that station in the contest. After a contact you started with a

  and RR73 and 73 carry it whole. They went out with the base call, another station's, and a reply
  to your call now moves the contact on. Not yet tried on the air.
- **Illinois QSO Party.** A station on a county line takes one entry: type its counties joined by
  `/` as the station sends them (up to four), or by `+` the way N3FJP takes them, and Enter logs one

  you export. Each contest contact records the operator at the key.
- **I moved works in the QSO parties.** In the Illinois, New York, California, Ohio, Tennessee and
  Texas parties, a mobile or rover presses **I moved** on the log strip and types the new county, by

  sends and logs the new county, a station worked from the old county is a new contact again, the
  Cabrillo file carries each contact's own county, and the new county becomes your County in
  Settings, so a restart keeps it. A station outside the state moves to another state or province
  the same way. I moved says no, and why, to a county across the state line (a separate entry) and
  to a county line: on a line, send the counties yourself and log each contact once per county,
  switching between them with I moved.
- **Contest logs keep what they were.** Merging a contest into your logbook never adds a contact
  twice, after I moved, a County changed in Settings or a restart, and a logbook edit or delete of a
  merged contact reaches the contest log. Each contact keeps the exchange it was sent with, so a
  County changed in Settings no longer changes contacts already logged. A contact logged more than
  four days after a contest's first ones, and the same contest a year later, are merged too.
  Restoring a settings backup that starts a contest checks your logbook for dupes, as a save does.
  The upload ticks under the merge show the services' names.
- **A contest's log holds every contact of that contest, however long ago it was logged.** It
  loaded only its last four days, so a QSO party opened a week later held none of the party's
  contacts. The Contest screen, the Cabrillo and ADIF exports, the score and the dupe check now see
  the whole contest, so a log exported a week or a month later is complete, and a station worked
  days ago is a dupe. Another contest's contacts, and the same contest next year, stay out of it.
- **A dupe is logged, marked and worth no points, in every contest, as N1MM Logger+ does.** At ARRL
  Field Day, Winter Field Day and the California, Illinois, Ohio, Tennessee and Texas QSO parties,
  Enter on a dupe in the CW, Phone or RTTY log strip logged nothing, and on FT8 or FT4 a dupe you
  double-clicked, replied to from a logger, or that answered your CQ was worked on the air and logged
  nowhere, which can cost the other station its credit. Now it is in the contest's log, marked as a
  dupe: the QSO count and the score do not change, and the Cabrillo and ADIF files, the merge into
  your logbook and club sync all keep it. A QSO party's club file keeps a repeat as a zero-point
  line; a Field Day club file keeps the earliest contact only. The dupe warning still shows while
  you type, S&P still passes a station you have worked, and with Enter Sends Message on, a dupe that
  calls your run gets QSO B4 once and the next Enter logs it, while in search and pounce Enter on a
  dupe still sends nothing; the Log button logs it.
- **A contest contact you never merged is never lost.** This computer's contest journal keeps every
  contact until it is in your logbook (it used to drop them four days on), and the Contest screen
  says how many, from which contest and when they were logged, with a button that merges them.
  Pressing it twice adds nothing twice, and a contact you took back stays taken back.
- **Nexus Remote shows a contest log that holds a duplicate**, with the repeat marked, where the
  Contest screen went blank.
- **A contest that is not on the list can log as one with the same exchange.** The guide shows the
  Scandinavian Activity Contest (RS and a serial number, each station once per band) logged as CQ
  WPX: pick it in Settings ▸ Contesting ▸ Contest and turn on Contest mode, then, before you send
  the log, change the Cabrillo file's `CONTEST:` line to `SAC-SSB` or `SAC-CW` and check its
  `CATEGORY-` lines against the SAC's own categories. The score Nexus shows is WPX's, and the
  sponsor works out its own.
- **Settings ▸ Contesting is in the order you set up an entry.** First the contest, with the
  Contest mode switch and Check for rules updates; then Your station data, what you send; then
  **Your entry**, what your log declares (Entry category, Power category, Spotting assistance,
  Station type, the Unassisted entry switch and its record, Email, Entry class, Club and Other
  operators); then Super Check Partial and call history, and Enter Sends Message; then **Field
  Day**, shown only while ARRL or Winter Field Day is picked; then the club. Every setting keeps its
  value. The Station tab's State and the contest's State or province say which is which.
- **The rest of a contest entry's Cabrillo declarations.** Your entry offers what a sponsor's
  Cabrillo template asks for beyond the entry category, each only for a contest whose sponsor has
  it: a multi-operator entry's class, written with the OPERATORS line such an entry must carry; an
  overlay (Classic, Rookie and Youth in CQ WW, for one); your mailing address, for the CQ contests,
  Sweepstakes, the VHF contests and the New York, Illinois and Tennessee QSO parties; and a soapbox.
  Nothing is written until you set it, so every contest's file is what it was. The Score Summary
  also lists QSOs by band and mode, for ARRL Field Day exactly as item 18 of ARRL's summary sheet
  lays them out, and for every other contest in its own modes.
- **CQ WW and CQ WPX Cabrillo files carry the category, club, name and email lines CQ asks for**,
  as the sponsors' own Cabrillo pages show them, each from your Settings and never guessed:
  CATEGORY-ASSISTED from Spotting assistance, CATEGORY-POWER from Power category, CATEGORY-BAND from

  multi-operator entry's class and operators), the Contest screen names it before you export, with

## Club sync and the scoreboard

- **Club sync runs the contest you picked.** A club event uses the host's contest and its rules:
  its exchange, what counts as a dupe (CW and digital are one mode at the Illinois QSO Party, and a
  mobile in a new county is a new contact), its multipliers and bonus stations, and its own Cabrillo
  file. At a QSO party the log strip warns while you type when another position has already worked
  the station from that county. Every position must pick the host's contest; one logging another is
  turned away, and its club chip names both. Club sync does not run a contest with a serial number
  in the exchange (Sweepstakes, CQ WPX, the California QSO Party) or CQ World-Wide, and says why.
  Try a club on two of your own PCs before the party; the Field Day manual has a checklist.
- **A joining laptop says whether it is in.** Under Join event at in Settings ▸ Contesting, and on
  the Contest screen, with Contest mode on or off, it says whether it is looking for the host,
  joined (with the event, the host's call, its position and its club code), turned away (in the
  host's own words), unable to reach the host (with what to check), or has lost the host and is
  trying again. An address typed without a port, such as 192.168.1.10, joins port 42073. The host
  says it is hosting, on which port and at which of its addresses, each with its network adapter's
  name, and how many positions are connected, or why it cannot host. Find club events listens for 5
  seconds and finds a host with more than one network adapter, and a click on an event it found
  joins it at once, saving that address and nothing else, with the line saying where the join
  stands right under the list (or, for a contest club sync cannot run, the reason). Not yet tried on
  two Windows laptops.
- **Allow Nexus through Windows Firewall.** On Windows, the club section of Settings ▸ Contesting
  has a button that asks for administrator rights once, as Repair clock does, and adds three
  inbound rules for Nexus itself on Private and Public networks: the Host port (TCP 42073 unless
  changed), Find club events (UDP 42074) and the TV scoreboard's port (TCP 7373 unless changed). A
  line beside it says whether they are in place, and a second button removes them. Where Windows
  blocks Nexus with a rule of its own, or blocks every incoming connection, the line says so. A
  press while you transmit is refused, because Windows' prompt takes the whole screen. Not yet tried
  on Windows.
- **Ctrl+D works at a club.** With club sync on, Ctrl+D twice (or Remove last) takes your newest
  contact back from the club too: the host keeps it, marked taken back and never deleted, every
  laptop's club dupe check and the club board drop it, and the club Cabrillo and the club's counts
  leave it out. Restore on the contest screen puts it back everywhere, and the club file is then
  exactly what it was. A laptop away from the host catches the club up when the link is back. Each
  laptop takes back only contacts it logged itself. Club sync moves to a new version for this, so
  every laptop in the club needs this release.
- **The scoreboard is made for a TV across the room**, for any contest club sync runs. The claimed
  score is the biggest thing on it, with how it is made, then the rate, the contacts by band and
  mode, each position's band and mode, the multipliers, the bonus stations, the latest contact, the
  clock and the time left. The map shows what the contest counts: the sections for the two Field
  Days, and the state's 102 counties for the Illinois QSO Party, each lit when the club works it. It
  is dark by default; add `?theme=light` to its address for a light board, or `?theme=auto` to
  follow the TV. A TV on a position shows the host's board (turn on Spectator scoreboard on the host
  too, on the same port), and when the host can't be reached the TV says so, keeps the last board
  and comes back by itself.
- **Clocks.** Each position checks its clock against the host's every 5 seconds and says so from 2
  seconds off, and the host's club board has a **Clock** column. Nexus never sets a clock and FT8
  and FT4 timing is untouched, so put a wrong clock right in that PC's own date and time settings.
- **Positions are turned away by name** when a laptop is on another callsign (not at ARRL Field
  Day, where the GOTA station uses a call of its own), has another state in its station data at a
  QSO party, has a Callsign on the air that is not a call sign, or claims a position another laptop
  already holds. Each laptop proves its position with a key its Nexus makes the first time it
  starts, and the host keeps each position for the event, through a restart; a new Event name
  starts afresh. Each laptop's club block shows its **Club code**, and the host shows the code of
  each laptop it turned away. A laptop whose whole settings folder was copied from one already
  connected is turned away by name and told how to get a position of its own, and two positions
  with one name read apart on every board, the TV and Remote. Both the laptop and the host say
  what to change and where, and a laptop logging another contest is told both contests by the
  names the contest picker uses. A contact the club's file cannot carry stays out of the club log,
  listed on the host, and stays in that laptop's log.
- **The host can give a club position to the laptop it belongs to.** A laptop turned away because
  another laptop holds its position (one that lost its club key file, say) has **Give this laptop
  its position** on its line in the host's club block. It shows that laptop's club code and asks
  you to check it against the one on that laptop's own Contest screen, and **The codes match: give
  it the position** gives it: the laptop joins on its next try and sends the club its whole log,
  and the laptop that held the position is turned away by name. Its contacts leave the club's log,
  and every laptop's club dupe warning, at once, and come back only if that laptop rejoins with a
  position of its own or the position is given back. A wrong press is undone the same way, and the
  host keeps the change through a restart. Only on the host's own screen, never through Remote.
- **The host sees where each position's link stands.** Its club board, on the Contest screen and in
  its pop-out, has a Status column: Connected while a position has been heard in the last 10
  seconds (each one sends a heartbeat every 5), Reconnecting once it misses its heartbeats, and Lost
  once 15 seconds pass and the host closes its link, each with when the host last heard it. The
  host's "other positions connected" line counts the same Connected lines, and a joined laptop's
  club line says the same of its link to the host.
- **The host can remove a position, and let it back.** Remove, on each other position's line of the
  host's club board, asks first, then closes that laptop's link and turns it away each time it
  tries again, until the host presses Allow back; the laptop's own screen says the host removed it.
  The contacts it already sent stay in the club's log, score, Cabrillo and ADIF, the ones it logs
  meanwhile go up when it is let back, and a removal is kept through a restart of the host. Every
  position that has joined has its line there, however many: once the club has more positions than
  each position's own board can show, the host's board still lists them all. Not yet tried on two
  laptops.
- **The club board shows every position in a band × mode grid.** Above the positions table, on the
  Contest screen, in the logger window, in the board's pop-out and on the hosted Remote page, each
  position's name sits in the cell of its band and mode: the contest's bands across, every one the
  same width so an empty cell is a free band and mode, and the contest's own mode classes down
  (CW/DIG and PH at the Illinois QSO Party, which counts CW and digital as one mode; CW, DIG and PH
  at Field Day). Two positions on one band and mode share the cell, a band the contest does not use
  gets a column of its own, and a position with no band yet is named under the grid. On the host's
  screen each name is coloured by its link, green Connected, amber Reconnecting and red Lost, with
  ✓, ↻ or ✕ beside it and a legend under the grid, and the Status column's Reconnecting and Lost
  read in the same amber and red. A position the host removed is left out of the grid; its line in
  the table keeps Allow back. A joined laptop and the hosted Remote page are not sent the host's
  link words, so no name is coloured there, and a position the host has not heard for more than 15
  seconds is greyed with ⚠, as its line in the table is. Each laptop marks its own name ⌂. When
  the bands do not fit the window, the grid scrolls sideways by itself, never the page.
- **ARRL Field Day: GOTA contacts go in the club's Cabrillo under the GOTA call**, as the rules
  require, and stay that way after the host restarts.
- **A rehearsal's contacts stay out of the contest.** Entering Contest mode loads only the contest
  you are entering, and only its contacts from 24 hours before it starts, so a club that rehearses
  midweek starts the party with clean logs. Nothing is deleted, and the contest screen says how
  many it kept out.
- **A big club no longer locks every position out.** A club of about 59 positions fits, and the
  host warns before one more might not.
- **A contact queued while a position was offline keeps the operator who logged it.**
- **Club sync says why it will not run** a contest the downloaded rules left out, by the contest's
  name, with where to check for a rules update.

## Winter Field Day 2027

- **The sponsor's 2027 rules.** Winter Field Day 2027 is on 23 and 24 January. The contest screen
  shows the thirteen 2027 objectives where ARRL Field Day shows its bonuses: tick each one you
  complete, or mark it planned. The score everywhere, the Cabrillo's claimed score included, is the
  QSO points × (objective multipliers + 1). For six bands, twelve bands and multiple modes a hint
  from your log says how far you are, and a note says when the QRP objective and your Power
  category disagree.
- **A contact through a satellite counts for nothing**, as the 2027 rules say. It is logged and
  kept, and reaches your logbook and LoTW when you merge the contest log, but it scores nothing and
  stays out of the Winter Field Day Cabrillo and ADIF files. A log of satellite contacts alone is
  saved.
- **No spots over the internet while the event runs.** The rules allow spotting only over RF, so a
  station with Winter Field Day switched on sends no PSK Reporter reports, cluster spots or POTA
  self-spots, from the desktop or from Nexus Remote. Receiving spots carries on, and everything goes
  back to normal when the event ends.
- **The Cabrillo header follows the sponsor's example:** your class and category, the power line
  for QRP or LOW (the limit is 100 W PEP), and your name, the email for contest logs, the club and
  the operators where you have set them.
- **The words follow the 2027 rules.** The class box is **WFD Class and Category** (H, I, O or M),
  FT2 is on the banned-mode warning, and the Field Day manual has a 2027 checklist, the 1 March
  deadline included.

- **The Rotor box points the antenna at the station in your log entry.** Its second line shows the

- **The Rotor and Amplifier boxes draw their readings bigger.** In the dashboard rail the Rotor

- **POTA/SOTA fetches once a minute per window**, however many lists show it, and Refresh in any of
  them updates all of them.
- **Solar wind** (Bz, Bt, speed and density) updates again, from NOAA's real-time feeds, after NOAA
  retired the two feeds Nexus read.
- **Map labels read in the light theme.** On the APRS map, Connect's maps and the Globe, each call
  sign, satellite name and DXpedition mark is outlined to suit its lettering, white under dark
  letters, where the light theme drew them as dark blots. The dark theme looks exactly as before.

## Radios and CAT

- **The status bar names the problem.** A PTT the radio did not accept, a sound card that failed, no
  receive audio, a headphone monitor held off and the rest each say what they are (PTT NOT
  ACCEPTED, SOUND CARD FAILED, NO RECEIVE AUDIO, HEADPHONE MONITOR OFF and so on), where all of them
  read RADIO STOPPED. RADIO ENGINE STOPPED means Nexus's radio engine itself has stopped and needs a
  restart. Only what stops receiving or transmitting flashes, and hovering the chip shows the full
  message.
- **Icom network (LAN / Wi-Fi), Beta.** For the IC-7610, IC-9700, IC-705, IC-905, IC-7760 and
  IC-7300MK2, Settings ▸ Radio ▸ Rig & CAT ▸ Connection has a new choice. Nexus logs in to the
  radio's own network server, the one RS-BA1 and wfview use, with no program in between, and follows
  frequency and mode both ways, the meters and the radio's panadapter. **Nothing transmits on this
  connection yet**, and there is no audio over it, so keep the USB cable for audio. The network
  password is kept in your computer's keychain. The radio takes one network program at a time; use
  it on your own network or through a VPN. USB stays the default. Not yet tried on a radio.
- **wfview's rigctld, and any rigctld on this computer.** With Rig Model NET rigctl and a Network
  Address on this computer, Nexus talks to the rigctld there itself, whatever rigctld TCP Port says,
  and reads wfview's replies, so it follows the dial, tunes and keys PTT through it (Rear/Data
  keying the DATA input). While nothing answers there yet, the CAT status says to start wfview or
  your rigctld and Nexus asks again on its own, so wfview and Nexus can start in either order. A
  second radio on NET rigctl, and a Remote selection of it, work the same way. Save refuses a change
  that would put another rigctld, or the Share this radio port, on that address, and says which port
  to change. A setup an earlier version saved that way is refused when it opens, for the radio you
  operate, one in the radio switcher and one picked from Remote: nothing is sent to that address, a
  key included, the CAT status names the radio or the port to change, and changing it connects at
  once. Test CAT and Auto-test show the same reason. Not yet checked with wfview on a real radio,
  started before Nexus and after it, nor with a settings file from 1.17.0 that holds such a clash.
- **CAT that could not connect at start is tried again on its own**, after 10 seconds and then less
  often (at most 5 minutes apart), and says what each try found. A retry never cuts an over on the
  air, and no longer turns transmit off in the digital modes. Not yet checked on a radio keyed by
  VOX whose CAT is not answering, across a retry in FT8.
- **No reply is not a refusal.** On native CI-V, OmniRig and the Flex native client, a frequency,
  mode, filter width or split change the radio does not answer (switched off, or a reply lost on the
  CI-V bus) says "no reply from the rig" and gets its tries, then Nexus stops asking and shows where
  the radio really is. A radio that answers NG is still reported as refusing. On Hamlib's rigctld an
  unanswered frequency or filter width gets three tries too, where it was sent again on every pass,
  which could hold up an over due to start. After an unanswered mode change the advice fits the
  connection, and an Icom switched off on native CI-V is sent nothing until it answers again. Not
  yet checked on a radio.
- **No guessed frequency range.** Native CI-V, OmniRig and the Flex native client no longer refuse a
  frequency from a range Nexus guessed: the radio answers for itself, so an IC-905 can take a QO-100
  downlink, and an IC-7300 or a FLEX-6400 asked for 2 m says the radio refused it. Your licence
  privileges are still checked. Not yet checked on a radio.
- **IC-905 on 10 GHz:** the dial, a QSY, the log (3 cm) and the panadapter are on the frequency the
  radio is on. Not yet tried on an IC-905 on 10 GHz.
- **Icom panadapters:** the IC-7610's strongest signals show at their real height, and Scroll-C
  sweeps, and a lower edge below 0 Hz, are drawn over the span the radio shows. Not yet checked on a
  radio.
- **The Data mode picker (D1/D2/D3)** is offered only on the IC-7610 and IC-7760, which have three
  DATA modes. The IC-9700, IC-705, IC-905 and IC-7300 are always sent their one DATA mode on
  Nexus's own CI-V, and where Nexus's own CI-V does not run, the picker is greyed. Not yet checked
  on an IC-9700, IC-705 or IC-905 that had D2 or D3 picked.
- **Fake It split puts the dial back after every over.** With Split operation set to Fake It, Nexus
  moves the radio to its TX frequency for an over and back after it. When the radio did not take
  the move back, Nexus never sent it again and then took the TX frequency for your own QSY, so the
  dial walked 500 Hz off FT8 (the band readout said "custom") and every later over walked with it.
  Now the move back goes 100 ms after the unkey, as WSJT-X does, and again up to three times in
  all if the radio does not take it, the over's TX frequency is no longer taken for your own QSY
  (one case is left: a frequency you set during an over can be taken for a moment from a radio slow
  to answer, or kept if the radio refuses it), and the CAT status says so if the radio still has not
  gone back. Turning the radio's knob is still followed.
  Not yet checked on an IC-7300 or IC-7300 MK2.
- **Rig split on Nexus's own Icom CI-V sends FT8 from a data mode.** With Split operation set to
  Rig, the transmit VFO was never put in a data mode, so FT8 went out in whatever mode that VFO
  was left in. Nexus now sets it to USB with the radio's DATA mode before each over (D1, D2 or D3
  on the IC-7610, as Settings' Data mode says), as it already did through Hamlib. Not yet checked
  on an IC-7610.
- **The shared radio address reports transmit however the radio was keyed:** Phone PTT, Tune, CW,
  RTTY, SSTV, another program's key, and the radio's own mic within about a second, so a script that
  mutes your computer's audio while you transmit works on SSB too. During a recorded voice message,
  an APRS packet and the last word of a CW message it still reads receive.
- **Help:** the Connection help points RS-BA1 users at Serial and the virtual COM port RS-BA1
  creates, and the setup wizard calls Network "SDR software, or a remote rig", as Settings does. The
  CI-V bus diagnostic log is offered by the radio model itself.

## FT8 and the digital modes

- **No CW ID after an FT8 or FT4 contact, as in WSJT-X.** With CW ID after 73 on, Nexus keyed your
  call in CW after its final 73 in FT8, FT4, FT2, TempoFast and TempoDeep; WSJT-X never sends the ID
  in FT8 or FT4. It no longer does, and since the ID turned TX back on, Disable TX after sending 73
  now leaves TX off after those contacts. Q65, FST4 and MSK144 send the ID as before, and the setting
  keeps its value.
- **A contest picked in Settings no longer stops FT8 QSO mode and Chat.** With a contest picked
  whose exchange was not filled in, switching to FT8 QSO mode to call CQ or to search and pounce,
  or to Chat, was refused with that contest's complaint, even with Contest mode off. With Contest
  mode off they always work; only the contest's own Running and S&P need its exchange, and they say
  what is missing.
- **Decode (F6) no longer holds the transmitter keyed.** The decode runs while the part of Nexus that
  keys and unkeys the radio carries on, so Decode pressed near the end of an over no longer keeps
  the radio keyed until it finishes. Working a station, Call CQ, a logger's Reply, a band, mode or
  radio change, an ATU tune-up and the end of a Tune also take effect at once, where a long decode
  could hold them up.
- **After a band or mode change, the decodes of the period being decoded show**, as in WSJT-X,
  under a line naming the band, mode and dial they were heard on, and ALL.TXT records them at that
  dial. They are for reading only: a double-click does not work them, they go to neither your logger
  nor PSK Reporter, and the auto-sequencer never answers them. As in WSJT-X, a7 decodes are hidden
  for one and a half periods after the change.
- **A mode switch in the middle of a QSO turns TX off when your Tx frequency leaves the waterfall**,
  as WSJT-X does. FT8 to FT4 on 20 m moves the dial from 14.074 to 14.080 MHz, and the next over no
  longer goes out in FT4 to a station still on FT8. The same goes for FT2, Tempo Fast and Tempo Deep.
  The QSO stays where it was. A switch that keeps your Tx frequency on the waterfall, Tempo Fast to
  Tempo Deep, a switch into WSPR and a turn of the VFO knob leave TX as it was.
- **FST4 and FST4W** no longer write past the end of the audio they make for an over, which could
  crash Nexus later on Windows and macOS. The audio sent is unchanged. Not yet checked: an FST4 and
  an FST4W transmit on Windows into a dummy load.
- **JS8's ✓ marks and the band map's struck-through calls** no longer blink when a new station is
  heard.
- **Repair clock no longer comes back every few minutes on a clock that is right** (Windows). The
  button under the clock chip could reappear at every clock check, about every ten minutes: on a
  Windows not set to English, when Windows Time's latest check had not taken although it had
  synchronised, and after any clock step, a repair's own included. For those it now shows only
  while the clock is more than a second off, when the clock chip turns amber. A stopped Windows
  Time service, or one that has never synchronised, still gets it. A repair Windows refuses part
  way now says "The clock was not repaired" and the button stays, where it said "Clock repaired".
  Not yet checked with the Windows administrator prompt accepted and declined.

## FlexRadio

All of this is on the Flex native client (Beta), and none of it is checked on a Flex radio yet.

- **Native DAX audio transmits on a radio already set to DAX**, as SmartSDR's own DAX switch leaves
  it. Before, Nexus refused every FT8 or other digital over there and turned TX off.
- **No silent overs.** When Flex native DAX audio goes off just before or during an FT8 or other
  digital over, or you press PTT as you switch from a digital mode to Phone, Nexus holds the over
  back or ends it, turns TX off and says why in the status lane, and the radio has its mic back
  within a moment.
- **The status lane says why Nexus held back an over** while the radio is not yet taking its
  transmit audio over DAX, instead of pointing you at your PTT method and CAT port.
- **An amplifier in line**, such as a PowerGenius XL that the radio names while an over keys, no
  longer turns the over's end into an alarm and a dropped connection.
- **No reply is not a refusal** here either: a mode, filter, power, AF, CW speed or noise switch the
  radio does not answer says there was no reply from the rig.
- **CW, Tune and the radio's own ATU through the Flex native client are built but switched off**
  until they have been checked on a radio. Until then the CW screen says the client sends no CW and
  that SmartSDR CAT does (turn the client off for CW, or use the WinKeyer or Soundcard keyer), Tune
  keys the radio and plays Nexus's tone as before, and the client offers no ATU button.

## Logging and confirmations

- **LoTW brings your whole confirmation history.** A first download asks for everything, and
  **Download everything again** (Settings ▸ Logging & Connectors ▸ Confirmations ▸ LoTW) asks
  first, then fetches the whole history once, for an account set up in Nexus before this release. A
  LoTW download also recognises every upload LoTW holds, so those contacts no longer wait for LoTW
  to take them. eQSL's first download asks for your whole InBox. Your logged contacts are not
  changed.
- **A confirmation goes on the contact it confirms.** If you worked a station twice on one band in a
  UTC day, a LoTW, eQSL or QRZ confirmation of the later contact could land on the earlier one and
  count toward awards. Each confirmation, and each upload LoTW holds, goes on the contact nearest its
  time, within 30 minutes, the window LoTW itself matches in.
- **Check confirmations** in the Logbook finds the confirmations earlier versions put on the wrong
  contact. It downloads your history once from LoTW, eQSL and QRZ, lists each contact under its
  service with the reason, and ticks only the ones that service's records decide. Nothing changes
  until you press Change and nothing is uploaded. The contacts it changes are first saved to a file
  beside your log, which Import ADIF puts back, and a paper card is never touched.
- **Sync from QRZ takes only QRZ's own confirmation**, never QRZ's copies of what LoTW, eQSL and
  paper cards say, and no longer marks a contact as uploaded to LoTW. The contacts this affects go
  in your next LoTW upload.
- **Pull confirmations automatically no longer blames your QRZ API key when nothing is new.** When
  nothing in your QRZ logbook had changed since the last pull, QRZ answered that no contact
  matched, and Nexus read that as a refusal: the Connection log said "QRZ rejected the FETCH —
  check your Logbook API key" every five minutes, and the last pull time stopped moving. A pull
  that finds nothing new is now a pull that worked. The same answer reads the same way elsewhere:
  Sync from QRZ on an empty QRZ logbook reports no new contacts, Check confirmations says that
  logbook holds no contacts and checks nothing, and correcting a contact at QRZ that QRZ does not
  hold says QRZ has no matching record. Not yet checked against a live QRZ account.
- **Loading a profile leaves your upload switches alone:** QRZ, ClubLog, eQSL, HRDLog.net, World
  Radio League, Cloudlog/Wavelog and LoTW, and the switches that send contacts to Ham Radio Deluxe,
  N3FJP, N1MM+ and DXKeeper.
- **ClubLog uploads work in the Linux and Raspberry Pi packages**, which carry the ClubLog
  application key as the Windows and Mac installers do, so your ClubLog email and Application
  Password are all you need. ClubLog's Most Wanted order is back in the DXpedition list there too.
  (not checked: only the release build's packages can show it)
- **"New mode" counts each mode you log on its own** on every screen, as the callsign card did: the
  Call Roster, Band Activity, the Needed board, the need alerts, POTA and SOTA activators and the
  DXpedition cards. USB and LSB still count as one mode, SSB, and the DXCC award totals still count
  CW, Phone and Digital, as ARRL does. A station heard in WSPR, or another mode no contact is logged
  in, counts by its kind (see Your screen).
- **Imports keep their mode.** A contact from JS8Call reads as JS8, and one from fldigi as PSK31,
  PSK63, QPSK31 and so on, and each is still written back exactly as it came in. Contacts imported
  before keep their old mode, and importing the same file again adds nothing twice. Changing a
  contact's mode drops a submode the new mode does not have.
- **On a slow disk** the contact you just logged shows within seconds in the Logbook, the Needed
  board, Awards, Journey and Statistics, and none of them answers without it. The Logbook says
  "Reading the logbook…" until your contacts are in, says when it could not read your log, with a
  **Retry** button, and the globe waits to count your grid squares.

## Nexus Remote

- **The stream tries only addresses a viewer could really be at.** The station no longer sends
  connection checks to an address a browser names on the station's own computer, on the local
  link (such as a cloud's metadata address) or for a whole group of computers, and it tries at
  most 16 of one browser's addresses and takes at most 64 in all. A browser on your own network or
  on Tailscale still connects directly. Not yet tried with a real browser against a station.
- **Through the stream, the Contest screen exports nothing, restores nothing and merges nothing.**
  A press of Export Cabrillo, Export ADIF, Summary, Dupe sheet, Club Cabrillo, Club ADIF, Restore
  or Merge … into my logbook that comes through the stream does nothing and says so ("Only at the
  station: …"), as Remove already does. The same press at the station works as before. Not yet
  tried through a real stream.
- **"Station data unavailable" after every loss of the station's data.** When the readings stopped
  just as a background read of the decode history gave up, the page refused its controls, as it
  should, but never faded or said so, however long the loss lasted. It now does, five seconds into
  every loss.
- **The contest view names the contest:** "Refresh contest", and "No contest is running on the
  station." when Contest mode is off there.
